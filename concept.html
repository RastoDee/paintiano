The concept

Music has colour.
It always had.

Twelve notes, twelve colours, one wheel. That is all of Paintiano.

Liebestraum — Liszt · the opening bars

Tone and colour are the same wave

Sound is a wave in the air. Light is a wave in a field. Only forty doublings separate them: concert A raised forty octaves stops being heard and starts being seen — as a warm red.

The whole system stands on this bridge: hue carries the pitch, lightness the octave, saturation the force of the touch. A bass C is deep blue, a soprano C almost white; forte is saturated, pianissimo barely a shadow.

Five views of one wheel

Twelve notes can be laid on a colour wheel by more than one rule — and each is true in its own way. A musician hears the circle of fifths. A physicist sees the spectrum. A painter looks for complements. Nature builds by the golden angle. The fifth view is yours.

There is no one right answer. There are four true ones — and one of your own.

Try them on the wheel above. Same piece, different mapping, different painting.

Two directions

Music → painting

Play, upload or hum. Every chord is a brushstroke; the same song gives the same painting, every time. Twenty-four painters each see it their own way.

Painting → music

The rule runs backwards too. Load a photo, a Guernica or a child's drawing — and Paintiano plays it. Twelve composers recompose it in their own voice.

Golden-ratio geometry

The canvas format and the basic cell of every painting come from φ = 1.618 — the ratio nature uses from the sunflower to the galaxy. Not because it is pretty. Because it is the same ratio the ear feels as consonance.
