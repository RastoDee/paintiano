It goes both ways

Music becomes painting.
Pictures become music.

Music → painting

A MIDI or MP3, sheet music, a built-in piece. Every note is a colour on the circle of fifths, every chord a brushstroke — placed by the golden ratio. The export carries the song's name and its colours.

Picture → music

Any photo or painting. Scan reads its colours as a score, or one of fifteen composers recomposes it in their own voice — The Scream picks Beethoven and Schubert, trading phrases.

✦ Signature

One painting that is the song's own.
The same for everyone.

Paintiano reads the piece — tempo, register, tension, major or minor — and casts a fusion of painters by roles: structure from rhythm, strokes from melody, colour ground from harmony. Play it on another phone, in another country: the same painting.

Liebestraum again — now by Pollock, Kandinsky, Hokusai, Rothko… Each painter has 6–9 ways of painting, and each one reads the song for real: the same notes, another hand.

The concept

“Music always had colour. We just forgot.”

Newton tuned the spectrum to the octave; Scriabin heard keys as colour. Paintiano builds the bridge for real: twelve notes on one wheel with twelve colours, laid out on the golden ratio. Neighbours in harmony are neighbours in colour — so a chord you can hear is a harmony you can see.
